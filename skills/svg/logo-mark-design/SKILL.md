# Logo Mark Design

## Core principle

A mark must survive its hardest conditions: a 16px favicon, a single-color print, and
a glance from someone who has never seen it. Design for those first, then add richness.

## Operating procedure

1. **Start in one color.** Design the silhouette in solid black on white. If it is not
   distinctive and readable in monochrome, color will not save it. Color comes last.

2. **Work the silhouette.**
   - The outline should be recognizable and distinctive.
   - Avoid generic symbols (a swoosh, a lightbulb, a generic gear) unless the concept
     genuinely requires it, and then make the form specific to the brand.
   - Test the silhouette with the interior filled - does it still make sense?

3. **Use negative space deliberately.** Meaningful negative space (a shape hidden in the
   gap between forms) creates memorability. Ensure it is intentional and legible at
   target sizes, not accidental.

4. **Balance geometrically.** Distribute visual weight evenly. A mark that looks like it
   will tip over reads as unstable. Center by optical weight, not bounding box.

5. **Test scalability.**
   - Design at a small size first (e.g. 32px), then check it works at 16px.
   - Provide a simplified variant where necessary for the smallest sizes; do not just
     shrink the detailed version.

6. **Confirm monochrome and reverse performance.** The mark must work as solid black,
   solid white on a dark background, and (if the brand requires) as a single accent color.

7. **Keep it simple.** Fewer elements scale better and are more memorable. Remove
   anything that does not contribute to recognition.

## Quality criteria

- Recognizable and distinctive in solid monochrome.
- Legible at the smallest required size (e.g. 16px favicon).
- Balanced visually and optically.
- Negative space is intentional.
- Works on both light and dark backgrounds.

## Anti-patterns to avoid

- Relying on gradients, shadows, or fine detail for recognizability.
- Complex illustration that collapses at small sizes.
- Generic clip-art symbols with no brand-specific form.
- Marks that only work in the one color they were designed in.
- Text baked into the mark in a way that breaks at small sizes (keep type as a
  separate logotype where possible).

## Required disclaimer

This skill produces design guidance and candidate marks. It does **not** provide:

- Trademark clearance or any assurance that a mark is legally usable.
- A guarantee that the mark is unique or not already in use.
- Legal advice of any kind.

For anything intended for real-world use, a trademark search and legal review are
required. State this explicitly rather than implying the mark is cleared.

## Validation strategy

- Render the mark at 16/32/128px and in monochrome; inspect. If no renderer is
  available, state that visual verification was not performed.
- Confirm it reads on both light and dark backgrounds.
- Confirm the silhouette is distinguishable when filled solid.
